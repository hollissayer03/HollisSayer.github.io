TECHNICAL DESIGN

Gameplay systems & implementation

Sight, hearing & movement noise

The enemy sensing logic responds to seeing the player and hearing player-generated noise. The diary records sight testing by approaching the monster, then sound testing with debug messages for noise generation and enemy hearing. This connects movement behaviour to a detectable signal rather than treating stealth as distance alone.

Insert sight and hearing gameplay comparison clip here.

Crouching: speed versus noise

Crouch movement was set to 250, one quarter of regular movement speed in the documented version. The implementation added an input action and movement-function changes, followed by a crouch check in noise reporting. The design gives the player a deliberate choice between faster movement and quieter traversal.

The diary credits a first-person crouch tutorial as the starting point; integrating crouch with the project's noise system is documented separately.

Insert walking versus crouching gameplay clip here.

Soldier pursuit, facing & reference checks

The soldier compares its distance to the player to switch from following to shooting. Development exposed a boundary-comparison issue and rotation that was not updated continuously. Later debugging isolated a reference problem when hearing and sight were both active; the diary records using the first-person character reference and adding IsValid checks before custom events.

Shared alerts between soldiers were attempted but recorded as unsuccessful. They are not presented here as a completed feature.

DESIGN DECISIONS

Level flow, teaching & route choice

The layout divides the experience into three stages: a tutorial, a mutant escape, and a lab breach. Each stage introduces a different pressure before the final area asks the player to combine timing, cover and route planning.

Stage 1 — Teaching through encounters

The tutorial plan uses a light and a fleeing mouse to draw the player toward a tunnel. The mouse reacts to sight and sound, giving a behavioural cue for crouching. Low cover reinforces the need to crouch; the first patrol introduces the threat. A knife reward and an enemy facing away create an opportunity to try a close-range kill, followed by a dropped note explaining the lights.

Stage 2 — Mutant escape

The intended opening reveals both the mutant spawn and a light, framing a narrow escape toward safety. Scattered lights let the player reconsider forward or backward movement depending on the mutant's position. The diary later records changing the hiding mechanic to lights that affect only the monster.

Stage 3 — Three ways to breach the lab

The quick entry route leaves more stealth challenges inside the base. The middle route balances outside and inside traversal. The longest approach reduces time inside for players who find the interior harder. These routes trade approach length against the type and amount of stealth required, rather than offering equivalent paths.

Encounter vocabulary

The encounter sketches explore progressively sparse cover, opposing patrol directions, short timing windows and tunnels that restrict peripheral vision. These constraints vary how the player reads and times patrol movement.

ITERATION

Problems found & changes made

Countdown UI: one source of time

Problem: Re-entering the out-of-bounds area restarted the displayed countdown while the underlying timer retained elapsed time. After four seconds outside, the UI could show ten seconds even though only six remained.

Change: The diary records replacing the UI's separate countdown logic with a reference to the timer in the out-of-bounds Blueprint and displaying a float value.

Recorded result: The diary reports that the UI now handled the countdown correctly. This is a development record, rather than a new test of the current build.

Hiding checks: avoid restarting behaviour every frame

Problem: Checking whether the player was hiding on Event Tick caused the monster to repeatedly free-roam on the spot.

Change: The hiding branch was moved into the follow-player custom events. A hiding player returned the AI to its prior behaviour; otherwise it continued following.

Later design revision: The hiding method was changed to a light that affected only the monster, tying the system more closely to the level design.

From greybox to a tighter playable space

The early greybox established building rooms, watchtower spacing and proposed enemy paths. Later entries record removing unnecessary map space, changing map size again, revising patrol-and-hide puzzles and adding walls to guide the player. The screenshots show development stages; the documents do not establish that every difference is the result of a measured playtest.

The debugging entries highlight the importance of shared state for UI, valid character references and placing behaviour checks where they do not restart continuously.
